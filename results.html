The reading experiment (stage 1, 2026-10-11)

The premise test: can text-only models actually read a scene from its text render? Sixty mazes with exact ground truth, 300 closed-form, purpose-shaped questions (goal bearing, adjacent walls, wall to the west, distance, does a path exist), each answer derived from the truth programmatically — never from a model's opinion. Every reader answered under three conditions: its own frame, no frame (blind), and another maze's frame (shuffled — the control that separates reading from prior knowledge of mazes).

Accuracy over 300 questions per cell, strict frozen scoring. Sensitivity, stated not buried: both readers left many blind answers unparsed (template echoes instead of guesses); counting only parsed answers, the honest lift range is +0.11 … +0.29. The shuffled condition scoring above blind for both readers shows why the blind control alone would overstate reading: a wrong-but-real maze transfers maze-family priors.

What the numbers teach

  • Position is genuinely read. Goal bearing: GLM 0.517 with the frame vs 0.017 blind, collapsing to 0.083 on shuffled frames — it is reading this frame. Of its 60 bearing answers, 31 were exact and 28 more had exactly one axis right. It localises; imprecisely, not lost.
  • Nobody verifies. In half the mazes the goal was sealed inside walls on all four sides, plainly visible in the frame. Asked "does a path exist?", GLM scored 0/30 and MiniMax 1/30 on those — while scoring ~100% on the open ones. Both asserted connectivity unconditionally; neither inspected the goal's neighbourhood. A walled-in goal went unnoticed 59 times out of 60.
  • Counting and local facts are not there yet. On adjacent-wall count, both readers lost to the constant answer "two" (0.533 / 0.300 vs 0.850). Distances were estimated, not counted — GLM's exact-hit rate was 0.067, with errors out to ±18 cells; MiniMax counted, with slips (0.183 exact, many off by one).
  • The proposed gate was not met. The architecture plan proposed stage-1 gates of ≥90% accuracy and ≥40 points of lift. Best result: 44.3% and +29. Per the plan, thresholds are now re-set from this baseline rather than from hope — and the curriculum implication is specific: teach verification behaviour and counting procedure, because positional reading is already there to build on.

Full write-up, with quoted failures and the harness: the experiment's RESULTS document. The teaching response is already written into Track 3 of Learn.

The kernel's receipts

  • 219 checks, 0 failures (suite re-run and verified locally, 2026-10-11).
  • Golden hash: fnv1a = 0x6dbdd1a8 — the kernel's output on its reference input is byte-identical on every machine; that hash is how you know.
  • WASM build: 6,808 bytes. The live demo runs it on your camera, in your browser, twice per frame (WASM plus an independent JS port) and counts byte-identical frames: syzygy-1j5.pages.dev.
  • Repo law, quoted because it governs this whole site: "Syzygy measures; it does not judge."

What is not true yet

Taken from the kernel repo's own gap list, kept current on purpose:

  • No microcontroller has actually run the kernel — six targets compile clean, none has been witnessed on metal.
  • The tokenizer is not learnable yet: it is a fixed argmax over a fixed basis, with no training harness. Teaching it is the current phase of the program.
  • The performance bench is drawn, not measured — the table is a claim awaiting a witness.
  • The mesh merge is proven in memory; its network transport (UDP) is unbuilt.
  • The reading experiment's readers were hosted models. The small local student — the 7 ms end of the design — is not trained yet.
